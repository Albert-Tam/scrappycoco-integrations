# LinkedIn and demand: useful content into relevant conversations

The CMO owns LinkedIn profile positioning, content strategy, post drafts, calendars,
public comments/replies, publishing and content performance. Reuse the company
brief; publishing, messaging and activation follow the skill's mandate.

Inspect the live LinkedIn research capabilities in [capability guidance](research-capabilities.md).
They acquire evidence; they do not publish posts or edit profiles. Publication,
profile edits and scheduling need a verified client tool and authorization covering
the prepared action. With no connector, deliver finished assets and identify the
remaining execution step. A proposed calendar is not a scheduled campaign.

## Give attention somewhere useful to land

Make the profile explain who the person helps, what changes for the buyer, why
they are credible and where to go next. Align the headline, banner, description
and featured material. Use a real work sample when no measured outcome exists;
a large number is not a prerequisite for credibility.

Positioning pattern:

> **[Work you do]** for **[specific audience]** facing **[recognizable problem]**.
> See **[actual example]** or **[clear next action]**.

Check the mobile presentation and available profile features. Prefer one clear
next path and relevant proof over competing offers.

## Write for buyers and the people who help them decide

Identify both the buyer and the practitioner who would forward a useful post.
Their questions come from sales conversations, onboarding, support, actual work
and product use. Use only material available for this company, and remove private
details before publication. Never invent an interview, client conversation or personal experience.

Audit a manageable recent sample, such as the last twenty posts. For each, record
the intended reader, topic, purpose, format and observed response. Inspect whether
reactions come from relevant buyers, internal advocates or mainly peers. Reach
without buyer conversations may indicate the wrong topic, audience or next step.

Study the company's own strong posts, relevant peers and adjacent fields. Compare
each example with its author's usual performance. Examine the mechanism: useful
specificity, clear explanation, distinctive evidence or a timely question. Treat
it as a hypothesis to test. Borrow structural ideas while writing original text
from the company's own experience; do not reuse another creator's assets or story
as if they were yours.

## Keep the content mix small

Choose a few recurring buyer problems and formats the team can sustain. Each post
has one main job:

| Job | Useful material | What to inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach relevant people | Clear viewpoint, timely explanation, honest story | Relevant new readers and profile visits |
| Make expertise usable | Walkthrough, checklist, comparison, worked example | Substantive questions, saves and resource use when observable |
| Demonstrate delivery | Actual output, process, case study, limitations | Buyer questions and qualified conversations |

Do not discard a strong proof post solely for lower reach. Personal and team
stories can build familiarity, but should not crowd out buyer problems. Scale
through distinct contributor expertise; a shared brief should not produce twenty
copies of the same voice. No compulsory contest, posting quota or manufactured
engagement is needed.

Use a cadence the team can keep while responding thoughtfully. Reuse a good idea
in another format when that format teaches it differently. Visuals should carry
meaning: a diagram for a process, a readable screenshot for proof, a demonstration
for something that needs to be seen. Text is suitable when it explains the idea
well. Do not present algorithm tricks or fixed posting times as guarantees.
Deliver publishable drafts using available assets and supported claims. If a
recording would help but is not available, provide a complete text version now
and treat the recording as an optional enhancement. Do not invent demo results
or ask the user to create an asset before supplying usable content, unless that
asset is essential to a format they explicitly requested.

## Draft with a clear promise and useful payoff

Fill this brief before writing:

```text
Reader and problem:
Post's main job:
One useful idea:
Actual example or evidence:
Promise in the opening:
Qualification or doubt to address:
Steps, demonstration or comparison:
One suitable ending:
```

Write the substance, then sharpen the opening. The first line explains the useful
outcome; the next adds necessary specificity or addresses an obvious objection.
Deliver the promise in the body. Cut throat-clearing and repeated points. Use
short paragraphs or labeled steps when that helps a partial reader learn.

Original draft structures; fill brackets with verified material:

**Practical walkthrough**

> “Before automating **[workflow]**, check **[specific failure point]**.
> Otherwise, the same error reaches every record.
> Here is the check: **[steps]**.
> In this example, **[actual input]** becomes **[actual output]**.
> Use it when **[condition]**; it will not solve **[limitation]**.”

**Proof with context**

> “Here is the **[deliverable]** we built for **[permitted client description]**.
> The constraint was **[real constraint]**.
> We changed **[specific mechanism]** and observed **[supported outcome]** over
> **[period]**. These were the other conditions: **[context]**.
> This is the part another team could test: **[step]**.”

**Honest lesson**

> “I expected **[belief]** to work. In **[real situation]**, it failed because
> **[observed reason]**. We tried **[change]**. Now I check **[lesson]** first.”

A lesson does not need a dramatic turnaround. Omit numbers or quotes you cannot
support. End with a useful conclusion, answerable question, relevant resource or
direct next step. Choose one. Build a promised resource before offering it, and
make the post useful without requiring the reader to comment for the answer.

## Continue relevant conversations

When engagement data is available, preserve person/profile identity, post/topic,
interaction type, actual comment and date. Deduplicate people while retaining their
separate interactions. Check company fit, role, relationship and current account
owner before spending on contact enrichment or drafting outreach.

A reaction suggests topic interest; it does not establish a business problem,
budget or permission to pitch. Repeated substantive exchanges can justify more
attention, but do not override poor fit. Group by a few relevant topics, then
prioritize by relationship and account value rather than creating dozens of
microscopic campaigns.

For an actual comment on the user's post:

> “Your question about **[specific issue]** deserves a fuller answer. The step
> I left out was **[useful detail]**. Is **[relevant condition]** part of your setup?”

For a suitable unfamiliar prospect, let the resource stand on its usefulness;
do not claim they read your work or suffer a problem merely because they reacted
elsewhere. Existing deals and conversations go to their owner. Coordinate with
VP of Sales for qualification and recipient-specific sales outreach, preserving
source evidence, exclusions and conversation history so replies stop overlapping
sequences. Keep LinkedIn public comments and replies with the CMO.

For events, distinguish registrants, attendees and no-shows. Deliver promised
materials and use the session topic accurately. Newsletter opens and clicks can
include machine activity; do not claim someone read an issue from those alone.
Respect the actual subscription and contact scope rather than treating every
subscriber as a sales prospect.

## Add distribution only when it serves the goal

Thoughtful comments on relevant discussions, co-created sessions, search articles
answering recurring buyer questions, and partner referrals can extend useful work.
Give each a defined audience and next step. Track qualified conversations and
eventual outcomes, not only distribution volume.

For paid account targeting, reuse the sales account definition. Specify audience
purpose, inclusion, exclusions, owner, creative, authorized budget and measurement
window. Exclude customers and active opportunities from generic acquisition unless
the campaign deliberately serves them. Use current destination requirements for
data eligibility, identifiers and audience minimums; a CRM import alone does not
establish first-party provenance.

Enrich only required missing fields. Check matched counts, removals and last sync;
an uploaded audience is not an active campaign. Company-level ad engagement does
not identify an individual viewer. Judge paid support through qualified outcomes
and comparable cohorts, accounting for conversion lag and avoiding double-counted
revenue. Launch and ongoing refresh require actual tools and the user's mandate.

Return the smallest useful package: profile changes if needed, a compact topic
plan, finished drafts and relevant follow-up drafts. Review which themes generate
buyer questions and mature opportunities, then adjust the next batch.
