# Outbound and pipeline: research, useful messages and replies

Use for qualified lists, campaigns, response diagnosis and replies. Reuse the
brief, exclusions, approved claims and permissions. Aim for relevant conversations.

## Build a list you can explain

Define the buyer, must-have fit, geography, exclusions, requested fields, bounded
input and spend limit. Inspect a representative sample before scaling acquisition.
Apply the installed Scrappycoco operational skill: the calling client judges
evidence, selects explicit provider configurations and uses cost to break a quality
tie. A successful API response is not a qualified account.

Use [research capabilities](research-capabilities.md) to inspect company and
employee database search/preview/collect, and jobs when hiring signals matter.
Do not omit these just because the first web search found a convenient directory.
Collect selected IDs in their original dataset and keep partial previews labeled.

Resolve company and person identities separately using stable identifiers. Match
existing records; suppress opt-outs and duplicate or incompatible outreach. Route
active opportunities to their owner.

Screen fit before buying contact fields. Enrich only missing or stale information
needed for the next action. Retain provider, date and native verification status.
Use a tested alternative for gaps within the budget; do not invent an email from
a naming pattern. Unknown, catch-all and conflicting results remain unresolved
and stay out of email enrollment until satisfactorily resolved. A verified address
does not guarantee delivery or inbox placement.

Return accepted, excluded and review-needed rows with reasons. Reconcile input,
duplicates, exclusions and gaps; processing N rows does not promise N prospects.

## Choose a reason relevant to the offer

| Starting evidence | Useful angle | Check before using it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual successful customer | Comparable problem and method | Same business model; authorized proof |
| Past champion changed company | Prior relationship, new responsibilities | Identity, current employer and new-company fit |
| Job, launch, leadership or technology change | Work the change may create | Event date, source and connection to the offer |
| Public complaint or stated question | Specific capability that addresses it | Current context; no inferred anonymous identity |
| Content/event interaction | Helpful continuation of that topic | Actual interaction, fit and existing conversation |
| Lost deal with changed blocker | Reopen the documented decision | Blocker really resolved; no guessing the “real” objection |

Local and specialist markets may need public directories, company sites or
industry sources. Verify the exact commercial activity that makes the offer fit.
No search result for a role is not proof nobody holds it; first detection of a
tool is not its installation date. Lookalike similarity is fit evidence, not intent.

For each signal save the event date, observation date, source, person/company
level and why it matters. Collapse one event reported by several providers into
one event. Set freshness according to the event and sales cycle; recheck stale or
contradictory data. Website or ad activity at company level can prioritize account
research, but cannot be attributed to a person found later.

Separate fit, timing, reachability and value. Match research effort to economics;
exclude weak fits from automated campaigns.

## Write from evidence, then make the next step easy

Use one checkable observation, an honest implication, useful value or proof, and
one next action. Write for a phone screen; omit irrelevant compliments, invented
familiarity and artificial urgency.

Before handing off a draft, read it as the recipient: why this person, why this
problem, what useful offer, and what easy reply? Keep a first email roughly 50–90
words unless the context needs more. Use one observation and one low-friction
question. Remove feature lists, internal architecture language, generic flattery,
and product-governance terms such as “native evidence” or “explicit configurations”
unless the recipient actually uses those terms. Translate capabilities into the
specific work the buyer could do. Do not assert an unverified pain or outcome.
If the message still works unchanged for every company, research or rewrite it.
Never present unfilled placeholders as a recipient-ready draft.

Illustrative offer for a technical buyer (adapt to verified context): “Would it
help to compare a couple of data providers on one of your actual tasks?” This
proposes useful work; it does not claim a comparison already happened or promise
savings. The opening must still come from that recipient's real workflow.

The following are original patterns. Fill placeholders only from evidence.

**Public hiring context**

> Subject: **[initiative]** at **[company]**
>
> Your **[role]** opening mentions **[specific responsibility]**. Is
> **[related problem]** part of that work? I put together **[actual useful
> resource]** covering **[relevant step]**. Would it be useful to send it?

**Useful work already completed**

> I checked **[public asset]** and found **[supported observation]**.
> Here is one possible improvement: **[concrete suggestion]**.
> I made **[actual sample]** to show how it could work. Want to see it?

**Relevant customer proof**

> We helped **[permitted customer description]** with **[supported outcome]**
> by changing **[mechanism]**. Your **[verified similarity]** made me think the
> approach might be relevant. Should I send the short case breakdown?

**Existing relationship or changed blocker**

> Last time we spoke, **[documented constraint]** ruled this out. Since then,
> **[verified change]**. Is it worth revisiting, or has the priority changed?

Use relationship copy only with evidence. Create a promised sample before claiming
it exists. Demonstrate on business information, not personal contact details in
a subject line. Answer direct buying questions directly.

Read the sequence together. Each follow-up adds value or answers a question:

> “One detail worth adding: **[new useful fact]**. This matters when
> **[condition]**. Does that match what your team is working on?”

End unanswered sequences without pressure; later contact needs relevance and scope.

## Coordinate channels and inspect the campaign

Choose channels by buyer behavior, contactability, relationship, value and capacity.
Connection acceptance is not buying intent. A reply on any channel pauses automated
follow-ups across the coordinated motion; an unavailable channel does not justify
guessing contact details.

Before a call, prepare role, reason, one useful point and a question:

> “Hi **[name]**, this is **[sender]** from **[company]**. I’m calling about
> **[public initiative or agreed topic]**. We help with **[relevant task]**.
> Is that something you handle?”

Prepare a concrete preview: recipients, filled variables, all steps, channel,
timing, reply owner, exclusions and stop conditions. Remove unsupported claims
and broken placeholders. Use available connector schemas; a CSV is not an upload,
and an uploaded draft is not a launched campaign. Reuse explicit authorization
already covering the action. Check uncertain execution before retrying.

## Handle the reply before expanding the list

Read the whole thread, including negation and prior context.

| Reply | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| Interested or asks a question | Answer it; propose a suitable next step |
| Not now, specific timing | Record the stated date and context |
| Referral | Clarify the introduction; verify the new role |
| Out of office | Send no automatic sales reply; respect return timing |
| No / not interested | Stop the current approach; retain the refusal |
| Opt-out | Suppress across the authorized outreach scope and verify completion |
| Ambiguous, angry or sensitive | Pause automation and surface the actual text |

“We use another vendor” is not automatically interest. Do not turn a polite refusal
into a nurture commitment. An original interested-response pattern is:

> “On **[their question]**, **[accurate answer]**. If useful, we can look at
> **[specific next step]** together. Does **[real available option]** suit you?”

Track the reply, owner, draft/action state and next step. Confirm booking and CRM
writes from tool evidence. Use [sales strategy](sales-strategy.md) for discovery,
proposal and delivery handoff; a positive reply alone is not a qualified deal.

## Diagnose before scaling

Inspect send failures, authentication evidence, provider responses, contact quality,
complaints and actual campaign performance. Separate campaign metrics from warmup
scores. Transport acceptance does not prove inbox placement; silence does not prove
spam filtering. Interpret errors with current provider documentation rather than
copying a fixed code table, DNS recipe or purported safe send quota.

Compare matching periods and mature cohorts. Separate replies from positive replies,
booked meetings from held meetings, and meetings from qualified opportunities.
Treat opens and clicks cautiously; automated scanners can generate them. Review
failure patterns by list, sender and recipient system without declaring a cause
from one metric. Return the evidence and a proportionate correction.

Feed real objections, fit mistakes and outcomes into the next bounded test. Preserve
the campaign version so the comparison is meaningful. Expand a promising segment
after quality and delivery hold; do not automatically refill a live campaign or
rewrite the targeting rules without the necessary mandate.
