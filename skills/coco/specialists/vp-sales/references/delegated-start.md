# Examples of a useful first handoff

Use these only to clarify the execution guide's method and scope rules. Adapt
them to verified source availability; they are not claims of connected tools or
results. Do not load this file on every request.

## Two ICPs, no method or quantity

User: “Find clients for Qolaig: EU forwarders using CargoWise, and pharma/biotech
transformation, operations, compliance or procurement leaders.”

“I can research these clients through three routes:
1. Job ads mentioning CargoWise to identify EU forwarders with evidence of use.
2. A B2B company and people database to find pharma/biotech firms and buyer roles.
3. Company websites and case studies to corroborate fit and tailor outreach.
I'd start with 1 and 2, bringing back two accounts per segment with buyers and
evidence before expanding. Shall I start there, or focus on one method?”

Name the actual database/platform when verified and useful. Do not infer pharma
geography solely from the user's language. Wait for the method choice unless it
was delegated. “Only method one” means the forwarder sample; acknowledge that pharma
is deferred. After the choice, do the sample without another start prompt. A
successful lookup is technical evidence, not permission to enrich 22 more companies.

Show real matches and assess them before recommending expansion, for example:
“The strongest matches are [actual segment], because [observed evidence]. I
recommend building a list of 20 accounts with one relevant buyer each. Shall I?”
Replace the brackets with evidence; never invent results to complete the pattern.

## Reuse the user's decisions

- “You choose how”: explain the source and method, then do a useful sample. No
  method-choice question; strategy delegation alone does not set research volume.
- “All three”: cover the selected methods in a small sample, not three campaigns.
- “Find 40 accounts”: explain the method, show representative quality, then finish
  the requested 40 without another continuation question when the method is known,
  delegated or straightforward.
- “Yes, build those 20”: execute the accepted expansion without restarting intake,
  method selection or sample approval.
- “Who is this company's COO?”: explain the lookup briefly and return the answer;
  do not manufacture a methods menu or sample stage.
- “Pick this up”: reuse the saved evidence and existing mandate. A sample awaiting
  expansion remains pending; silence is not acceptance.
