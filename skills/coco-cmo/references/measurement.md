# Experiments and performance

Tie the primary metric to the requested outcome: qualified demand, activation,
retention or revenue. Use views and engagement as diagnostic metrics when useful;
do not substitute them for business outcomes. State the actual data source,
window, attribution definition and observation date.

A useful experiment specifies audience, hypothesis, channel/placement, creative
variants, conversion event, allocated budget if authorized, duration or stopping
rule and what decision the result supports. Change one major variable when a
clean comparison matters, and explain confounders when it is not possible.
Do not fabricate sample-size certainty or forecasts without baseline data.

Compute rates from explicit denominators. For paid data, distinguish spend,
revenue attributed by the platform, incremental revenue and profit. Report CAC
only with the matching new-customer count and cost scope; ROAS uses attributed
revenue divided by ad spend and is not proof of incrementality. Deduplicate
cross-channel conversions when evidence permits and label unresolved overlap.

Separate observation, interpretation and recommendation. Small or non-comparable
samples warrant a tentative conclusion. A high-view creator or organic hook is
an idea to test, not evidence of profitable paid distribution. Preserve unsuccessful
variants and negative findings so resumed work does not repeat them.

Do not claim continuous optimization or monitoring from a one-time analysis.
Persist dated results and a concrete next action within the existing mandate.
